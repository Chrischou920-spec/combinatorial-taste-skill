# Compatibility and Composition Rules

Use after analysis, when choosing a direction and restructuring the page. The goal is to transfer design relationships into a coherent expression suited to the business.

## Establish Business Constraints First

Guide selection with one concrete goal and content priorities: who visits, what they must understand first, what earns their trust, and what they should do next. A brand homepage may emphasize its promise and credible evidence; a product page may emphasize the problem, capabilities, and action; a portfolio may emphasize representative work and the author's role. These are considerations, not a fixed section order. Reorder according to the actual business.

## Method Compatibility Table

For each candidate method, record source IDs, whether to retain, adapt, or discard it, the reason, and how to adapt it. Cover at least the following:

| Relationship | Compatibility conditions | How to resolve conflicts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typography × images | Letterforms and stroke weight fit the images' lighting, detail, and character; Chinese is supported | Preserve typographic roles rather than specific fonts; reset line height, letter spacing, and wrapping using real Chinese content |
| Density × spacing | Actual content volume, scanning needs, and section rhythm fit | Adjust grouping and priorities; do not remove core requirements to preserve an airy composition |
| Components × components | The same semantic roles use the same corner radii, borders, shadows, and icon language | Normalize into tokens; differences must represent explainable states or roles |
| Color × hierarchy | Text is readable; accent colors concentrate on important information and actions | Adjust color values or areas and check contrast; prevent every section from competing for accent colors |
| Decoration / motion × task | Helps explain, orient, or provide feedback without obstructing reading or operation | Remove unnecessary effects; retain a static treatment and a reduced-motion option |
| Method × actual content | Still works with long Chinese headlines, long paragraphs, and the available number of assets | Change proportions or image strategy, or discard the method; do not sustain the design with invented assets |

Compatibility means more than looking similar: different styles can be combined when their shared semantic roles and unifying rules can be explained. Prefer discarding an element if it requires many exceptions to work. Do not mechanically calculate each reference's contribution percentage.

## Candidate Directions and Selection

Develop two to three directions internally. Summarize each by character, structure, visual focus, image strategy, and key tradeoffs. They must differ in at least one of layout, density, narrative, or visual emphasis, not merely use three color schemes. Compare fit to requirements, capacity for content, compatibility, distinctiveness, and implementation constraints, then continue with the best fit. Even one reference can yield candidates through different business structures. If the user has specified a style, vary candidates within that constraint without expanding the task on your own.

## Reconstruction and Originality Check

Rebuild the content map, reading order, visual focus, and primary action, then map the selected methods onto the new structure. Do not substitute “R1 hero + R2 cards + R3 footer” for design. If the output still shares a distinctive overall composition, visual focus, assets, and consecutive section order with any reference, change the structural relationships further; changing colors and wording is insufficient.

Transferable methods include grouping through spacing, role contrast, alignment, cropping logic, and narrative rhythm. Do not directly reuse reference logos, copy, proprietary images, brand-specific illustrations, or an entire signature composition. Handle user-provided or permitted assets within their authorization scope; being able to view a reference does not authorize reusing its assets. If no images are available, prioritize text, factual charts, verifiable product content, or original graphics; do not fabricate photos or achievements.

Finally, use actual content to check whether the whole page works as one system, serves the business, and has its own expression. Neither the number of references nor the number of replaced colors proves originality.
